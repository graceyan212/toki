Questions

Are these recordings of the real switches?

No — each one is modelled from scratch and generated as you type. They are our own emulations, named for the switches they were voiced against; Toki isn't affiliated with or endorsed by any switch maker. That is why no two keystrokes are identical: a sampled app replays the same clip and starts to grate after a few hundred words. It is also why you can bend them with the dials above, which a fixed audio file cannot do.

So how close is it to the real thing?

Close enough that people who own these switches recognise them, not close enough to fool a recording. If you want a literal capture of your exact board, this is the wrong tool.

Will it annoy people around me?

It is your speakers, so it is as loud as you set it — and headphones make it private. You can also turn off the key-up sound, which roughly halves the number of clicks.

Does it record what I type?

No. macOS makes an app ask before it can watch for keystrokes, and Toki asks for the narrowest one there is — Input Monitoring, not Accessibility. It cannot read your screen or control other apps. The listener is listen-only: each key becomes a size and a stereo position, then is discarded. There is no logging path and no network code, so there is nothing to store and nowhere to send it. It also cannot see passwords — macOS hides those from every app, including this one.

Which keyboards work?

All of them. It listens to the keystroke, not the hardware, so your laptop's built-in keyboard works the same as an external board.

What do I actually install?

One menu-bar icon on macOS 13 or later, Apple silicon or Intel. No window, no dock icon, no account.
